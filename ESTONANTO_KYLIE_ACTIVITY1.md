**I.  Objectives**

At the end of this activity, you should be able to:

• Identify the major computing career pathways introduced in lecture and describe what each one involves.

• Choose one computing career pathway that best matches your interests and explain why.

• Map a realistic entry-to-expert roadmap for your chosen pathway, including representative job titles and key skills at each stage.

• Compare your current skills against your chosen pathway's requirements and identify a concrete plan to close the gap.

**II.  Materials Needed**

• Your CC 101 Unit 2 lecture slides/notes on Computing Career Pathways

• This worksheet (printed copy, or the digital file to fill out and submit)

• Optional: internet access, for Part E (real job posting search)

**III.  Background**

Computing is not just “programming.” As introduced in the Unit 2 lecture, the field branches into several distinct career pathways, each with its own operational scope, required technical competencies, representative job titles, and professional standard or reference body: Software Engineering, Systems & Cloud Infrastructure, Data Science & AI Engineering, Cybersecurity & Threat Defense, and Information Systems & Enterprise IT. Each pathway also has its own career roadmap — the entry-level, mid-level, senior-level, and expert/leadership roles a professional typically grows through. This activity asks you to review those five pathways, choose the one that interests you most, and map out what growing in that pathway
could look like for you.

**IV.  Procedure**

***Part A – Recall the Five Pathways***

1. Using your lecture notes or slides, complete Table 1 by writing the core operational scope and one to two representative job titles for each of the five computing career pathways.

***Part B – Choose Your Pathway***

2. From the five pathways, choose ONE that interests you the most.

3. In two to three sentences, explain why you chose it (e.g., skills you already enjoy, a subject you like, a job title that excites you).

**VI.  Realization (Reflection)**

*In two to four sentences each, answer the following in your own words.*

**1. Why did you choose this particular pathway over the other four?**

Because, as of now, I’m interested in developing my own software and applications with my ideas. It
is also versatile because I can eventually apply my foundations and skills to other fields. Another thing
is that this field can be my creative outlet when I want to make an idea work.

**2. Looking at your roadmap, which career stage excites you the most, and why?**

Senior-Level. Because I heard that senior-level positions have more opportunities for remote work
and good pay. They are also more comfortable due to their experience, and they know enough to not
stress over small details as much as someone at the entry level would. This would mean having more
responsibility, but the fact that you have gained more knowledge and experiences means that you
have the capability to work on more complex projects and turn your ideas into reality.

**3. Which required skill or competency for this pathway do you already have, and which one will be hardest**
**for you to develop?**

I have some basic experience with using objects through Visual Basic but I still aim to improve that
skill beyond the beginner level. The hardest for me would be Software Architecture. Not only does it
require you to apply everything, including Data Structures, but you will also face many decisions that
can affect your projects. Therefore, you will need a lot of experience and good judgement to make
the right decisions.

**4. How does mapping this roadmap change — or confirm — your plans for the rest of this course and your**
**degree?**

It confirms my plans, considering the roadmap gave me an overview of the skills I may need and what
I may encounter when choosing this path. I stand by the Software Engineering roadmap because of its
versatility and how well it aligns with my passion, which is turning my ideas into creative solutions to
solve real-world problems. I can always explore other specializations in the future, but at least I will
have the foundations I need for each of them.
